Analytical Or Research Question

Does a person’s number of days they used public transportation per month on average over the last 3 months predict their composite communication apprehension score from responses to questions about their confidence in conversations?

Answer, Response, & Summary of Results

Yes. The number of days that a person uses public transportation in a week is a strong predictor of their composite communication apprehension score from questions related to their confidence in conversation.

data_c <- data_c |> 
  mutate(days_str = as.factor(Q26))

m_graph <- lm(Q_Composite ~ days_str, data = data_c)

x <- expand.grid(days_str = c("Never", "0-1 days a month", "2-4 days a month", "2-4 days a month", "4-8 days a month", "8 or more days a month"))

preds <- predict(m_graph, x, se.fit = TRUE) |> 
  as_tibble() |> 
  mutate(upper = fit + se.fit,
         lower = fit - se.fit) |> 
  bind_cols(x)

ggplot(preds, aes(x = days_str, y = fit, fill = days_str)) +
  geom_bar(stat = "identity",  
           position = position_dodge(width = .5), 
           width = .5) + 
  geom_errorbar(aes(ymin = lower, ymax = upper), 
                position = position_dodge(width = .5), 
                width = .25) +
  labs(x = "Days in last 3 months", 
       y = "Total Commucation Apprehension Score",
       fill = "Days in last 3 months")

This graph shows the gradual progression in total communication apprehension across responses to the number of days a person has used public transportation per month over the last 3 months. In general, the trend as the number of days public transportation was used per month over the last 3 months is a decrease in total communication apprehension score, with the exception of an excess of 8 or more days per month over the last 3 months.

Remaining Questions + Uncertainties

It seems clear that the number of days a participant has used public transportation per month over the last 3 months reliably predicts their composite communication apprehension score. However, it is not yet clear how other variables can influence these predictions. How can variables like age, gender, and employment status affect these results?
